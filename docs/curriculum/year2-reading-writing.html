How the syllabus is organised

NSW groups Years 1 and 2 together as Stage 1. The outcomes describe what a student should be able to do by the end of Year 2, so this map is the target for the end of this school year and the start of next. English has nine focus areas. Six of them are directly about reading and writing; the other three support both.

Reading = decoding × understanding language

Reading needs two things together: working out the words on the page (phonics, spelling patterns, fluency) and understanding the language (vocabulary, sentences, background knowledge). Writing is the same in reverse, plus handwriting.

Deniz already speaks and listens well, which is a strong base for the "understanding" side. When a child speaks well but reads and writes poorly, the gap is often in the code: letter-sound knowledge, spelling patterns and automatic word reading. The assessment will confirm whether that is true for her. Vocabulary still needs attention, because written English uses many "book words" that rarely come up in conversation.

Suggested order of work for Deniz

All areas matter, but some skills unlock others. This order puts the foundations first. It is a starting plan and will change once we have her assessment results.

  1. Find her starting point. Quick checks of letter sounds, phonics patterns, high-frequency words, a short read-aloud and a writing sample.
  2. Phonics & high-frequency words. Fill gaps in the code: digraphs, vowel teams, r-controlled vowels. Daily and short (10–15 minutes).
  3. Fluency. Re-read short decodable and levelled texts until they sound like talking. Paired reading with a parent.
  4. Spelling & handwriting. Spell the same patterns she is learning to read. Correct letter formation so writing becomes automatic.
  5. Sentences, then short texts. Say it, then write it. Simple → compound → complex sentences, then recounts and opinions.
  6. Comprehension & vocabulary throughout. Read aloud to her from books above her reading level, talk about word meanings, ask "why" and "how do you know" questions.

  • Uses spelling strategies well; punctuation mostly accurate
  • Could add more detail about who and where

Year 1 Phonics Screening Check

NSW public schools check all Year 1 students in Term 3: 40 words (20 real, 20 nonsense) read one-to-one with the teacher in about 5–7 minutes. Ask the school whether Deniz did it in 2025 and what her result was. It is a useful clue to her phonics gaps.

EAL/D support at school

NSW places EAL/D (English as an additional language) students on a progression with four phases: Beginning, Emerging, Developing, Consolidating. Ask her teacher which phase she has been placed in and whether she gets EAL/D teacher support.
